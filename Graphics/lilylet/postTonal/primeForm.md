# Finding prime form: Bartók, Mikrokosmos No. 141, "Subject and Reflection" (right hand)

```lyl
[title "Bartók, Mikrokosmos No. 141: right-hand motive"]
[composer "Béla Bartók"]

\key c \major \time 2/4 \clef "treble" \stemUp bf'8[( c ef d] \\
\stemDown bf'2 | %1

\time 5/8 \partial 4 \stemUp f'4) \\
\stemDown bf'4 | %2
```

(1) Normal order: [10, 0, 2, 3, 5]

(2) Transpose to zero: [0, 2, 4, 5, 7]

(3) Invert (any inversion will work!) step 2 and put the inversion in normal order: [5, 7, 8, T, 0]

(4) Transpose to zero: [0, 2, 3, 5, 7]

(5) Finally, compare the results of step (2) and (4). Prime form is the version that is most compact.

[0, 2, 4, 5, 7] vs. [0, 2, 3, 5, 7]: the outer intervals are 7 and 5 in both; the first interval is 4 vs. 3, and 3 is smaller.

Prime form = (02357)
