# Modal Mixture (Modal Borrowing)

## Summary

Modal mixture refers to borrowing chords from a parallel key (e.g., using F minor chords in F major) without modulating. Like applied chords, borrowed chords add chromatic color while maintaining the home key.

## Core Concept

### Definition

- **Modal mixture** = using chords from the parallel major or minor
- **No modulation** = only a cadence can confirm a new key
- **Borrowed chords** = chromatic alterations within the home key

### What Is NOT Modal Mixture

In minor keys, these are "native" and do not constitute borrowing:
- Leading tone (ti) replacing subtonic (te)
- Melodic minor figure: sol-la-ti-do

## Roman Numeral Notation

### Same Root, Different Quality

When the chord root belongs to the home key but quality changes:

| Home Key | Borrowed Chord | RN | Example in C Major |
|----------|----------------|----|--------------------|
| Major | Minor tonic | i | C-Eb-G |
| Major | Minor subdominant | iv | F-Ab-C |
| Minor | Major tonic | I | C-E-G (Picardy third) |

### Altered Root

When the root is chromatically altered, use flat/sharp before RN:

| Alteration | Symbol | Meaning |
|------------|--------|---------|
| Lowered root | ♭ | Semitone below normal |
| Raised root | ♯ | Semitone above normal |

**Example:** In C major, le-do-me chord = **♭VI** (A♭-C-E♭)

## Functional Bass Notation

### Bracket Convention

Borrowed chords use **square brackets** to indicate chromatic alteration:

| Diatonic | Borrowed | FB Symbol |
|----------|----------|-----------|
| S4 (fa-la-do) | S4 (fa-le-do) | **[S4]** |
| T1 (do-mi-sol) | T1 (do-me-sol) | **[T1]** |

### Altered Bass Note

If the bass note itself is altered, use **+** or **-** before the numeral:

| Chord | Bass | FB Symbol | Example |
|-------|------|-----------|---------|
| ♭VI | le | **[Tx-6]** | le-do-me in major |
| ♭VII | te | **[D-7]** | te-re-fa in major |
| ♭III | me | **[T-3]** | me-sol-te in major |

## Common Borrowed Chords

### In Major Keys (borrowing from minor)

| Chord | Solfege | Function | Usage |
|-------|---------|----------|-------|
| iv | fa-le-do | S | Common, darker subdominant |
| ♭VI | le-do-me | Tx | Deceptive resolution, emotional |
| ♭VII | te-re-fa | D | Rock/pop, modal flavor |
| ii° | re-fa-le | S | Darker pre-dominant |
| ♭III | me-sol-te | T | Mediant relationship |

### In Minor Keys (borrowing from major)

| Chord | Name | Function | Usage |
|-------|------|----------|-------|
| I | Picardy third | T | Final cadence, resolution |
| IV | Major subdominant | S | Brighter color |

## Compositional Application

### When to Use Modal Mixture

1. **Emotional contrast** - Add darkness to major or brightness to minor
2. **Plagal enrichment** - iv-I cadence in major keys
3. **Deceptive motion** - V-♭VI instead of V-vi
4. **Rock/pop idiom** - ♭VII is standard in rock

### Common Progressions

```
Major key with mixture:
I - iv - I        (T - [S] - T)
I - ♭VI - ♭VII - I   (T - [Tx] - [D] - T)
I - V - ♭VI - IV     (T - D - [Tx] - S)  "Deceptive pop"
```

## Constraints & Rules

1. **Voice-leading**: Chromatic tones (le, me, te) should resolve smoothly
2. **No cadence in borrowed key**: Otherwise it becomes modulation
3. **Functional coherence**: Borrowed chord should fulfill same function as diatonic counterpart
4. **Thoroughbass adjustment**: Always alter figures to reflect chromatic changes
