# Applied Chords (Secondary Dominants)

## Summary

Applied chords temporarily emphasize non-tonic diatonic chords by borrowing dominant-function harmony from their key, without establishing a new tonic through cadence.

## Core Concept

- **Applied chord** = dominant of a diatonic chord (V/x or vii°/x)
- **Tonicization** = momentary emphasis without modulation
- **No cadence** in the borrowed key = still in home key

## Applied Chord Types

### Applied Dominant (V/x)

| Target | Applied V | Applied V7 | Chromatic Alteration |
|--------|-----------|------------|---------------------|
| V | V/V | V7/V | fa → fi |
| IV | V/IV | V7/IV | do → di (rare) |
| ii | V/ii | V7/ii | do → di |
| vi | V/vi | V7/vi | sol → si |
| iii | V/iii | V7/iii | re → ri, fa → fi |

### Applied Leading-Tone (vii°/x)

| Target | Applied vii° | Applied vii°7 |
|--------|--------------|---------------|
| V | vii°/V | vii°7/V |
| IV | vii°/IV | vii°7/IV |
| ii | vii°/ii | vii°7/ii |
| vi | vii°/vi | vii°7/vi |

## Scale Degree Tendencies

Each applied chord has characteristic chromatic scale degrees:

| Applied to | Scale Degrees in V/x |
|------------|---------------------|
| V | fi, la, do, (re), (me) |
| IV | mi, sol, (te), do, (ra) |
| ii | di, mi, sol, (la), (te) |
| vi | si, ti, re, (mi), (fa) |
| iii | ri, fi, la, (ti), (do) |

## Voice-Leading Rules

### Resolution Requirements

1. **Raised tone** (fi, di, ri, si) = temporary leading tone → resolves UP by step
2. **Seventh** of V7/x → resolves DOWN by step
3. **Diminished fifth** of vii°/x → resolves inward

### Functional Dissonance

In applied chords, judge dissonances relative to the TARGET key:
- The 7th of V7/x is *fa* of the target key → must resolve down
- The raised tone is *ti* of the target key → must resolve up

## Common Progressions

### With V/V

```
I - V/V - V - I        (T - [S] - D - T)
I - IV - V/V - V - I   (T - S - [S] - D - T)
```

### With V/ii

```
I - V/ii - ii - V - I  (T - [T] - S - D - T)
```

### With V/vi

```
I - V/vi - vi - IV - V - I  (T - [D] - T - S - D - T)
```

## Compositional Application

### When to Use Applied Chords

1. **Strengthen arrival** on important non-tonic harmony
2. **Add chromatic color** without modulating
3. **Create forward momentum** through raised leading tones
4. **Prolong subdominant function** (V/V before V)

### Frequency Guidelines

| Target | Frequency | Context |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| V | Very common | Pre-dominant intensification |
| ii | Common | Strengthens S function |
| vi | Common | Deceptive motion preparation |
| IV | Less common | Plagal contexts |
| iii | Rare | Usually in sequences |

## Functional Bass Notation

Applied chords are marked with brackets and slash notation:

| Chord | Roman Numeral | Functional Bass |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|
| V/V | V/V | [S+4] or [S2] |
| V7/V | V7/V | [S+4] |
| V/ii | V/ii | [T+1] |
| vii°/V | vii°/V | [S+4] |

## Constraints

1. **Applied chords must resolve to target** (V/V → V, not V/V → vi)
2. **Target must be major or minor triad** (no V/vii°)
3. **Raised tones resolve UP**
4. **Still functions within home key** (altered S, D, or T)
