# Neapolitan Chord

## Summary

The Neapolitan chord (N. or N.6) is a major triad built on lowered scale-degree 2 (ra). It functions as an intensified subdominant, typically appearing in first inversion with fa in the bass.

## Core Concept

### Construction

| Scale Degree | Solfege | In C minor |
|--------------|---------|------------|
| Lowered 2 | ra | Db |
| 4 | fa | F |
| Lowered 6 | le | Ab |

**Result:** Major triad on ♭2 (Db major in C minor)

### Standard Position

- **First inversion** (N.6) is standard
- **fa in the bass** (scale degree 4)
- **fa is doubled** in four-voice texture

### Function

Always **subdominant function** [S] — a chromatic intensification of pre-dominant harmony.

## Notation

### Roman Numeral

- **N.** or **N.6** — replaces Roman numeral
- NOT labeled as ♭II in classical analysis (though pop/jazz may use ♭II)

### Functional Bass

```
[S4]
 N.
```

The bracket indicates chromatic alteration; "N." labels the specific chord.

## Voice-Leading

### Standard Resolution

N.6 → V (or cad. 6/4 → V)

```
N.6        →    V
ra-fa-le   →    sol-ti-re
  ↓
ra → do → ti (♭2 moves to 1, then to leading tone in inner voice)
le → sol (down by half step)
fa → sol (up by step, or fa → fi → sol via cad. 6/4)
```

### Characteristic Motion

The hallmark voice-leading involves:
- **ra → do** (♭2 → 1) in an upper voice — the expressive chromatic descent
- **le → sol** (♭6 → 5) — standard resolution
- Often via **cadential 6/4**: N.6 → cad. 6/4 → V, where ra moves to sol (of cad. 6/4) then ti

## Compositional Application

### When to Use

1. **Dramatic pre-cadential moment** - More intense than standard iv
2. **Minor key climaxes** - Most common in minor
3. **Expressive/emotional passages** - Romantic era favorite
4. **Text painting** - Often used for grief, longing, darkness

### Common Progressions

```
Minor key:
i - N.6 - V - i       (T - [S] - D - T)
i - iv - N.6 - V - i  (T - S - [S] - D - T)
i - N.6 - V7 - i      (T - [S] - D - T)
```

### Intensity Gradient

| Chord | Intensity | Context |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| iv | Mild | Standard minor subdominant |
| ii°6 | Moderate | Common pre-dominant |
| N.6 | High | Dramatic, chromatic |
| Aug6 | Highest | Maximum tension before V |

## Musical Example

### Schubert: "Der Müller und der Bach"

*Die schöne Müllerin*, No. 19, mm. 7-10:

```
G minor: i → N.6 → V7 → i
         T →  S  → D  → T
```

The Neapolitan chord creates a moment of intense pathos before resolving to the dominant.

## Comparison with Related Chords

| Chord | Members | Bass | Function |
|-------|---------|------|----------|
| iv | fa-le-do | fa | S |
| N.6 | ra-fa-le | fa | [S] |
| ii°6 | fa-le-re | fa | S |

All three have **fa in the bass** but different qualities and intensities.

## Constraints

1. **First inversion standard** - Root position rare
2. **Double fa** (the bass note)
3. **Resolves to V** (directly or via cad. 6/4)
4. **Minor key preferred** - Major key possible but less common
5. **ra resolves to do (or sol)** - ♭2 moves down to 1 or up to cad. 6/4
