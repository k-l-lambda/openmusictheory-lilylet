# Species Counterpoint

## Summary

Two-voice species counterpoint after Fux (*Gradus ad Parnassum*, 1725): one new line against a whole-note cantus firmus (cf), with each species adding one rhythmic ratio and one kind of dissonance. v2 says it trains basic skills rather than one repertoire's style; the last sections give v2's rule-like guidance for 16th-century imitation and the high-Baroque fugal exposition.

## Rule Strength

v2 grades its rules by wording, and this file keeps that grading:

| Label | v2 wording | Meaning |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| **Strict** | must, never, forbidden, always, cannot, "make sure to avoid" | A violation is an error |
| **Pref.** | should, prefer, try, limit, "also try to avoid" | Style norm; break it only for a reason |

Where two v2 chapters grade the same rule differently, the table says so.

## Intervals (all species)

| Class | Intervals | Use |
|-------|-----------|-----|
| Perfect consonance | P1, P5, P8 (and compounds, e.g. P12) | Begin and end; sparingly inside |
| Imperfect consonance | m3, M3, m6, M6 (and compounds) | Preferred inside the exercise |
| Dissonance | All 2nds and 7ths; all augmented and diminished intervals; **P4** | The P4 is dissonant whenever it involves the lowest voice, which in two voices means always |

Motion types: contrary (opposite directions), similar (same direction), parallel (same direction, same interval type), oblique (one voice holds).

## Cantus Firmus

v2 lists these as the characteristics of every well-formed cf:

| Feature | Norm |
|---------|------|
| Length | About 8–16 notes |
| Rhythm | All whole notes |
| First and last note | *do* (modal final) |
| Approach to final | By step: usually *re–do*, sometimes *ti–do* |
| Melodic intervals | Melodic consonances only |
| Range | ≤ a 10th; usually < an octave |
| Climax | One high point, normally heard once |
| Motion | Mostly steps; some leaps, mostly small; no repeated motives or "licks" |
| Large leap (≥ 4th) | Followed by a step in the opposite direction |
| Consecutive leaps | No more than two in a row; none in the same direction (exception: Fux's F-mode cf, whose two descending leaps A–F–D outline a consonant triad) |
| Leading tone | Present in every mode (raised in "minor"-type modes); goes to the tonic |

Fux's cantus firmi in Part I (46 exercises in all; v2 02/07):

| Final | Pitches | Uses in Part I |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| D | D4 F4 E4 D4 G4 F4 A4 G4 F4 E4 D4 | 10 |
| E | E4 C4 D4 C4 A3 A4 G4 E4 F4 E4 | 10 |
| F | F3 G3 A3 F3 D3 E3 F3 C4 A3 F3 G3 F3 | 10 |
| G | G3 C4 B3 G3 C4 E4 D4 G4 E4 C4 D4 B3 A3 G3 | 6 |
| A | A3 C4 B3 D4 C4 E4 F4 E4 D4 C4 B3 A3 | 6 |
| C | C4 E4 F4 G4 E4 A4 G4 E4 F4 E4 D4 C4 / C4 D4 F4 E4 G4 E4 F4 E4 D4 C4 | 2 + 2 |

## General Melodic Rules (counterpoint line, all species)

| Rule | Strength |
|------|----------|
| Approach the final P1/P8 by step (clausula vera) | Strict |
| Limit repeated notes (consecutive and across the exercise), consecutive leaps (≥ 3rd; separate them with steps), leaps in a short span, and runs of the same generic interval | Pref. |
| Exactly one climax per part; don't end on it; preferably leap to it; the two parts' climaxes must not coincide | Pref. |
| No melodic leap of an augmented or diminished interval, any 7th, any 6th (except perhaps the ascending m6), or anything larger than an octave | Pref. in 02/01 ("avoid"); 02/02 makes tritone and 7th leaps **forbidden** |
| Don't outline (span with successive notes) an augmented or diminished interval or a 7th | Pref. |
| A large leap is followed by a step in the opposite direction | Pref. |

## General Harmonic Rules (all species)

| Rule | Strength |
|------|----------|
| First interval P1, P5 or P8; last interval P1 or P8 (never P5) | Strict |
| Space between the voices ≤ P12; mostly ≤ an octave; > a 10th only in "emergencies", for 1–2 notes | Pref. |
| No voice crossing or overlap (overlap: upper part on E4, so the lower part cannot take F4 in the next bar) | Pref. ("avoid") |
| No parallel 5ths or 8ves | Strict |
| No direct (hidden) 5ths/8ves: any interval moving by similar motion into a P5/P8 | Pref. in 02/01; **Strict** for 1st species in 02/02 ("never") |
| No unisons except the first and last intervals | Pref. (relaxed on weak beats in species 2–3) |

## Beginnings and Endings

| Species | Begin above cf | Begin below cf | Opening rhythm | Ending (clausula vera) |
|---------|----------------|----------------|----------------|------------------------|
| 1st | *do* or *sol* (P1/P5/P8) | *do* only (P1/P8) | Whole note | Penultimate whole note, then *do* |
| 2nd | same | same | Half rest + half note (preferred) or two halves | Penultimate bar: whole note or two halves; final *do* |
| 3rd | same | same | Four quarters, or quarter rest + three quarters | Last quarter of penultimate bar *ti*/*re*; final *do* as a whole note (Strict) |
| 4th | same | same | Half rest (**Strict**: "always") | Fixed formula below |
| 5th | Perfect consonance | Perfect consonance | Free | Clausula vera |

- Below the cf, *sol* is excluded because it makes a P4 with *do*. *fa* makes a P5 but sounds like *do–sol*, so it confuses the key.
- The clausula vera always uses contrary stepwise motion into the final *do* (Strict):

| cf ends | Counterpoint ends | Penultimate interval |
|---------|-------------------|----------------------|
| *re–do* | *ti–do* | Above: 6th → 8ve. Below: 3rd → unison (or 10th → 8ve) |
| *ti–do* | *re–do* | Above: 3rd → unison (or 10th → 8ve). Below: 6th → 8ve |

## First Species (1:1)

| Rule | Strength |
|------|----------|
| Every vertical interval is a consonance | Strict |
| Unisons only as the first and last intervals | Pref. ("should") |
| Inside the exercise, prefer imperfect consonances; vary the intervals | Pref. |
| No two perfect consonances of the same size in a row: P5–P5, P8–P8, P5–P12 all count | Strict ("never") |
| Two different perfect consonances in a row (e.g. P8–P5) are allowed; still follow a perfect consonance with an imperfect one where possible | Pref. |
| No more than three of the same imperfect consonance in a row (e.g. three 3rds) | Strict ("do not") |
| Never enter a perfect consonance by similar motion (direct 5ths/8ves) | Strict |
| Avoid combining similar motion with leaps, especially large ones | Pref. |
| Prefer contrary motion; use each motion type (oblique is optional) | Pref. |
| One climax per line; the two climaxes don't coincide | Pref. |
| At most one repeated note or tie; ideally none | Pref. |

## Second Species (2:1)

### By beat position

| Position | Allowed | Notes |
|----------|---------|-------|
| Downbeat (strong) | Consonances only (Strict) | Prefer 3rds and 6ths; avoid unisons |
| Weak beat | Consonance, or a dissonance that is a passing tone (Strict) | Unisons are permitted when they result from smooth voice leading |

### Motion rules

| Span | Rule | Strength |
|------|------|----------|
| Weak beat → next downbeat | All 1st-species rules apply (e.g. weak-beat P5 → downbeat P5 is a parallel) | Strict |
| Downbeat → downbeat | Don't begin two consecutive bars with the same perfect interval | Strict ("do not") |
| Downbeat → downbeat | No dissonant melodic interval between successive downbeat notes. Exception: an octave leap strong→weak followed by a step back, which spans a 7th | Strict, with that exception |
| Downbeat → downbeat | No more than three bars in a row beginning with the same imperfect consonance | Strict |
| Downbeat → downbeat | Hidden/direct 5ths/8ves are fine (the intervening note weakens them) | Allowed |
| Leaps | Leap within the bar (strong→weak) rather than across the bar line | Pref. |
| Line shape | More stepwise than 1st species; 1–2 secondary climaxes below the main one | Pref. |

### Weak-beat patterns (decision table)

Fix the downbeats first, then choose the pattern that fills the space between them. v2 says weak beats that fit none of these patterns probably signal an error.

| Pattern | Downbeat → downbeat (melodic) | Motion | Weak beat vs. cf |
|---------|-------------------------------|--------|------------------|
| Dissonant passing tone | 3rd | Step + step, same direction | Dissonant: the only allowed dissonance |
| Consonant passing tone | 3rd | Step + step, same direction | Consonant: always a P5 or 6th |
| Substitution | 3rd | Leap a 4th, then step back (opposite direction) | Consonant |
| Skipped passing tone | 4th | 3rd + step, same direction | Consonant |
| Interval subdivision | 5th (3rd + 3rd) or 6th (3rd + 4th or 4th + 3rd) | Two leaps, same direction | Consonant; all melodic intervals consonant |
| Change of register | Varies | Leap a P5, 6th or 8ve strong→weak, then step in the opposite direction | Consonant; use infrequently |
| Delay of melodic progression | Step | Leap a 3rd, then step in the opposite direction | Consonant |
| Consonant neighbor | Same pitch | Step away, step back | Consonant: P5 ↔ 6th (5–6–5 or 6–5–6) |

Corollary (Strict): never leap into or out of a dissonance, change direction on one, or put one on a downbeat.

## Third Species (4:1)

### By beat position

| Beat | Weight | Allowed |
|------|--------|---------|
| 1 | Strong | Consonance, not a unison (Strict); prefer imperfect |
| 3 | Medium | Consonance; or a dissonant passing or neighbor tone |
| 2, 4 | Weak | Consonance (unisons allowed if needed); or a dissonant passing or neighbor tone (neighbors are smoothest on 2 or 4) |

Mix consonances and dissonances on beats 2–4 for variety (Pref.).

### Motion rules

| Span | Rule | Strength |
|------|------|----------|
| Beat 4 → downbeat | 1st-species rules | Strict |
| Downbeat → downbeat | Direct 5ths/8ves allowed | Allowed |
| Downbeat → downbeat | No three consecutive bars beginning with the same perfect interval (two are fine) | Strict ("can") |
| Downbeat → downbeat | No more than three bars in a row beginning with the same imperfect consonance | Pref. ("should") |
| Downbeat → downbeat | No dissonant melodic interval between downbeat pitches | Strict ("must not") |
| Into a downbeat P5 | Beats 3 and 4 of the previous bar cannot be a P5 | Strict |
| Into a downbeat P8 | Beats 2, 3 and 4 of the previous bar cannot be a P8 | Strict |
| Consonant notes | Approach and leave freely, by step or by leap from another consonance; leaps must be melodic consonances; a large leap is followed by a step in the opposite direction | Strict / Pref. |

Note: 2nd species forbids even two bars in a row beginning with the same perfect interval; 3rd species allows two and forbids three.

### Dissonance figures

All third-species dissonances fall on beats 2, 3 or 4. They are approached and left by step, except in the double neighbor and the nota cambiata.

| Figure | Shape (beats) | Conditions |
|--------|---------------|-----------|
| Passing tone | Fills a melodic 3rd by step | The notes on either side are consonant. Two dissonant PTs in a row are allowed (P4–d5 or d5–P4) if the line moves stepwise in one direction and neither falls on a downbeat |
| Neighbor tone | Consonance, step away, step back (e.g. 6–7–6) | Best on beat 2 or 4 |
| Double neighbor | 1 and 4 the same pitch; 2 and 3 a step above and a step below (C–D–B–C or C–B–D–C) | Beats 2 and 3 are both dissonant. Beat 3→4 and beat 4→next downbeat go in the same direction, and the second of those moves is a step |
| Nota cambiata | Five notes: downbeat, step, leap a 3rd in the same direction, then two steps in the opposite direction, the second landing on the next downbeat (step-down form: C–B–G–A–B) | Notes 1, 3 and 5 are consonant; note 2 is dissonant and is left by leap. Note 2 equals note 5, the next downbeat, so the figure spans one step overall. It exists in step-down and step-up forms |

## Fourth Species (Suspensions)

The counterpoint moves in half notes, offset by a half note. Each weak-beat note is tied over the bar line, so in pure fourth species every move is oblique.

### Suspension figure

| Part | Beat | vs. cf | Rule |
|------|------|--------|------|
| Preparation | Weak | Consonant | Tied to the suspension (same pitch) |
| Suspension | Strong | Dissonant | Accented dissonance |
| Resolution | Weak | Consonant | One step **down** from the suspension |

### Suspension types

| Counterpoint | Dissonant suspensions | Status |
|--------------|-----------------------|--------|
| Above cf | 7–6, 4–3, 9–8 (2–1) | v2: the only dissonances that resolve down by step to a consonance. 7–6 and 4–3 are preferable because they resolve to imperfect consonances |
| Below cf | 2–3 | v2's "main" type |
| Either | 5–6 (and other consonant ties) | A tie, not a dissonant suspension |

Interval arithmetic: above the cf, a resolution down a step subtracts 1 (7→6, 4→3, 9→8, 2→1). Below the cf, the lower voice falls, so it adds 1 (2→3).

### Rules

| Rule | Strength |
|------|----------|
| Use dissonant suspensions as often as possible | Pref. (the point of the exercise) |
| Treat each suspension like its resolution interval in 1st species: 7–6, 4–3 (above) or 2–3, 5–6 (below) at most three in a row | Strict |
| Never two 9–8, 6–5 or 4–5 in a row (after-beat 8ves/5ths). More generally, no consecutive weak-beat 5ths or 8ves | Strict ("do not") |
| If no dissonant suspension fits, tie anyway (a consonant suspension), or leap up from a downbeat consonance to a weak-beat consonance | Pref. |
| Include 1–2 upward leaps to offset the falling resolutions | Pref. ("will be necessary") |
| Melodic shape and climax matter less than correct preparation and resolution | Pref. |
| Breaking species: only if nothing else works. Use 2nd-species rules, at most once per exercise, for a bar or two | Pref. |

### Fixed ending

| | Penultimate bar | Final bar |
|---|---|---|
| cf (must end *re–do*; never use a *ti–do* cf) | *re* | *do* |
| Counterpoint above | *do* (tied, a 7th) – *ti* (a 6th) = 7–6 | *do* (8ve) |
| Counterpoint below | *do* (tied, a 2nd) – *ti* (a 3rd) = 2–3 | *do* |

## Fifth Species (Florid)

| Rule | Strength |
|------|----------|
| Mix whole notes, half notes, quarter notes and tied suspensions in almost any order and combination | Allowed |
| Begin with a perfect consonance; end with a clausula vera; keep the first and last bars' motion reduced | Strict |
| Suspensions may be embellished. Sustaining the bar's first note as a half note must recover a correct 4th-species suspension | Strict (reduction test) |
| Eighth notes occur only in pairs, on weak quarters (2nd or 4th quarter of the bar). Types: a pair of passing eighths, or the "anticipation with turn" embellishment of a suspension | Strict |
| Balance the types of consonance and the types of species motion | Pref. |

## 16th-Century Style (v2 02/08)

v2 calls this related to, but not identical with, species counterpoint. Rules for completing partial scores:

| Area | Rule |
|------|------|
| Imitation interval | Usually a perfect consonance at the start of a section; others occur later |
| Imitation meter | Imitate at metrically comparable positions (strong → strong, weak → weak) |
| Imitation extent | Keep at least the rhythm and the distinctive intervals and contour of the point's opening; entries must overlap meaningfully (shorter points need closer entries) |
| Motion | Contrary motion predominates; avoid long parallels even of allowed intervals (exception: fauxbourdon, a run of parallel 6/3s) |
| Melodic steps and leaps | 2nds, 3rds, 4ths, 5ths and 8ves are allowed. Ascending m6 is used freely (814× in Palestrina's masses), ascending M6 is rarer (54×), descending 6ths are extremely rare (7 m6, 1 M6) |
| Forbidden leaps | 7ths and tritones, also as outlines (successive leaps or a gesture's high and low points) |
| Successive leaps | Avoid in the same direction. If used, put the larger leap lower (first when rising, second when falling) |
| Large leaps | Followed, and often preceded, by motion in the opposite direction; don't arpeggiate triads |
| Final | Approach by step in most parts; raise the leading note from below (not in Phrygian) |
| Range per part | Typically ~14 semitones; the 12th is the maximum |
| Parallels | No parallel 5ths or 8ves, including those involving a d5 |
| Direct 5ths/8ves | Avoid in principle; more common at cadences or when other parts move strongly in contrary motion |
| Diminished triad | Mostly in first inversion; resolve *re–do*, *ti–do*, *fa–mi* |
| Dissonance | Suspensions (prepared on a weak beat, dissonant on a strong beat, resolved down a step on a weak beat: 7–6 and 4–3 with the dissonance in the upper voice, 2–3 in the lower). Passing and neighbor tones at the quarter-note level. Nota cambiata *do–ti–sol–la*: the only dissonance left by leap |
| Final chord | Bare 5th or major triad (≈ 85% major in Palestrina's masses) |
| Rhythm | Quarter-note motion begins on weak positions; ties connect long notes to shorter ones, not the reverse; triple meter divides 2+1 |
| Text | At least one half note per syllable (the short note of a dotted rhythm may take its own); syllable changes right after quarter-note motion are rare |

The 5–6 oblique motion is also common, but it is not a suspension.

## Fugal Exposition (v2 02/09)

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Subject | The fugue's main tune. Often Kopfmotiv (head motive) + Fortspinnung (spinning-out, sequence or motor rhythm) + cadence |
| Answer | The subject transposed by P5/P4: **real** (exact) or **tonal** (adjusted) |
| Countersubject | A line sounding with, and complementing, the subject or answer |
| Link | A short interpolation between entries, often a sequenced subject motive |
| Redundant entry | An entry beyond the number of voices |

Entry pattern: voice 1 states the subject, then continues with the countersubject while voice 2 states the answer. Voice 1 then goes on in free counterpoint while voice 2 takes the countersubject and voice 3 enters with the subject.

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Entry register | Each new voice enters as the highest or lowest sounding voice. The 8 valid four-voice orders: SATB, BTAS, ASTB, ATSB, ATBS, TBAS, TABS, TASB |
| Entry meter | All exposition entries begin at metrically comparable positions (if the subject starts on a pickup, every entry does) |
| Tonal answer: when | The subject (a) starts on *sol* or uses it prominently at the outset, (b) suggests V–I at the start, or (c) modulates |
| Tonal answer: how | (a) answer that *sol* with *do*, not *re*. (b) swap T and D: prominent dominant notes become tonic notes and vice versa. (c) split the subject at the modulation; if the subject ends in the dominant, the answer modulates back to the tonic |
| Tonal answer: limit | Change as little as possible, so subject and answer stay alike and both make melodic sense |
| Countersubject, harmony | Write it for subject and answer together; it must outline clear harmonies (triads, 7ths) in two voices |
| Countersubject, invertible at the octave | Use unisons/8ves, 3rds/6ths, and 2nds/7ths in seventh chords. Avoid 5ths (they invert to 4ths) and leave the 5th to the free voice. Avoid 4–3 (inverts to consonant 5–6); use 7–6 and 2–3, which invert into each other |
| Countersubject, rhythm | Complementary: one voice moves while the other holds. Change it between subject and answer versions only where the subject and answer themselves differ |
| Links | Used to change harmony, reset register, vary phrase lengths, or dovetail an entry |

## Constraints

1. Two voices: the P4 is a dissonance.
2. Start on P1/P5/P8 (below the cf only P1/P8 on *do*); end on P1/P8 on *do* by contrary stepwise motion.
3. No parallel 5ths/8ves in any species, including across the bar line (weak beat → downbeat) and at the after-beat in 4th species.
4. 1st species: all consonant; no direct 5ths/8ves; ≤ 3 of the same imperfect consonance in a row.
5. 2nd species: downbeats consonant; the only weak-beat dissonance is a passing tone filling a 3rd.
6. 3rd species: dissonances on beats 2–4 only, as PT, NT, double neighbor or cambiata; downbeat P5 blocks a P5 on beats 3–4 of the bar before; downbeat P8 blocks a P8 on beats 2–4.
7. 4th species: preparation consonant (weak), suspension dissonant (strong), resolution one step down (weak). Above the cf: 7–6, 4–3, 9–8. Below: 2–3.
8. 4th-species cadence: cf *re–do*; counterpoint *do* (tied) – *ti* – *do* (7–6 above, 2–3 below).
9. 5th species: eighth notes only in pairs on weak quarters.
10. Melodic leaps: no augmented, diminished, 7th, or > octave; of the 6ths only the ascending m6 is possible.
11. Fugue: tonal answer whenever the subject begins on or stresses *sol*, implies V–I, or modulates; the invertible countersubject avoids 5ths and 4–3 suspensions.
