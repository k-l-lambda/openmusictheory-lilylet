# Counterpoint

## Summary

Counterpoint combines independent lines into consonant simultaneities. OMT v2 teaches it through Fux's five species for two voices (*Gradus ad Parnassum*, 1725), then applies it to 16th-century imitation and the high-Baroque fugal exposition.

## The Five Species (two voices)

| Species | Counterpoint vs. cantus firmus | New problem introduced |
|---------|--------------------------------|------------------------|
| 1st | 1:1 (whole notes) | Beginnings, endings, independence; consonance only |
| 2nd | 2:1 (half notes) | Strong vs. weak beat; dissonant passing tone |
| 3rd | 4:1 (quarter notes) | Medium beat; neighbor, double neighbor, nota cambiata |
| 4th | 1:1 offset by a half note, tied | Suspension (preparation, suspension, resolution) |
| 5th | Mixture of 1st–4th | Embellished suspensions; paired eighth notes |

## Shared Foundations

| Concept | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Perfect consonances | P1, P5, P8 (and compounds) |
| Imperfect consonances | m3, M3, m6, M6 |
| Dissonances | All 2nds and 7ths, all augmented and diminished intervals, and the P4 (two voices: every 4th involves the lowest part) |
| Motion types | Contrary, similar, parallel, oblique |
| Cadence | Clausula vera: contrary stepwise motion into a final P1/P8 on *do* |
