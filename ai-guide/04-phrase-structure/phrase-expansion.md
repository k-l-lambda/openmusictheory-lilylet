# Phrase Expansion

## Summary

Phrase expansion techniques extend phrases beyond their normative lengths. Internal expansions lengthen sub-phrases or delay cadences through repetition. External expansions add material before (prefix) or after (suffix) a theme.

## The Problem of Regular Phrasing

William Rothstein's "Great Nineteenth-Century Rhythm Problem": the danger of "too unrelievedly duple a hypermetrical pattern" — submitting to "the tyranny of the four-measure phrase."

Classical music often breaks this regularity through expansion techniques.

## Internal Expansions

### Sub-Phrase Expansion

**Definition:** Enlarging a 2-bar BI or CI through motivic repetition.

**Mechanism:**
- Motive is repeated (exact or embellished)
- Sub-phrase grows beyond expected 2 bars

**Example:** Mozart, Rondo K. 494, mm. 1-12
- Period with expanded BI (m. 3 repeats m. 2)
- Simple recomposition could restore 2-bar length

### Phrase Expansion

**Definition:** Extending a phrase, usually by repeating the concluding portion to delay cadential arrival.

**Common triggers:**
1. **IAC instead of PAC** — weaker than expected
2. **Deceptive cadence** — V-vi instead of V-I
3. **Evaded cadence** — expected cadence never materializes

### Expansion by Weak Cadence

**Example:** Beethoven, Piano Sonata Op. 2/3

```
mm. 1-8: Sentence, expects PAC
m. 8: IAC arrives (melody on scale degree 3)
mm. 8-13: Continuation repeats → achieves PAC
```

Result: 5 extra measures added to phrase.

### Expansion by Evaded Cadence

**Definition:** Expected cadence is avoided entirely — leading tone doesn't resolve.

**Example:** Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 309, III

```
mm. 1-16: Compound period expects PAC
m. 15-16: Cadence evaded (leading tone leaps away)
mm. 16-19: "One more time" → achieves PAC
```

## External Expansions

### Prefix

**Definition:** Material that precedes a theme, lacking distinct motive or harmonic progression.

**Characteristics:**
- Short (often 1-2 bars)
- Accompanimental character
- No melodic identity
- Removable without disturbing phrase logic

**Example:** Haydn, Symphony No. 101 "Clock"
- 1-bar prefix precedes 4-bar antecedent
- Pure accompaniment, no melody, no progression

### Suffix Types

| After | Name | Function |
|-------|------|----------|
| PAC | Closing section (codetta) | Confirms/prolongs tonic |
| HC | Standing on the dominant | Prolongs dominant for transition |

### Closing Section (Codetta)

**Definition:** Material following PAC that prolongs tonic harmony.

**Characteristics:**
- Melody centered on scale degree 1
- Cadential progression often repeats (ii-V-I)
- Confirms closure

**Example:** Mozart, Piano Sonata K. 330
- Compound period ends PAC in m. 16
- 5-bar closing section confirms cadence

### Standing on the Dominant

**Definition:** Suffix following HC that prolongs dominant harmony.

**Function signals:**
- Second theme entrance (sonata form)
- Recapitulation return
- Dramatic preparation

**Characteristics:**
- Emphasizes dominant harmony
- Scale degree 5 melodically embellished
- Creates anticipation

**Example:** Mozart, Symphony No. 40, K. 550, i
- Compound sentence ends HC in m. 16
- Following measures stand on dominant
- Prepares theme return

## Non-Standard vs. Expanded

**Key question:** Could the passage be "duple-fied" without destroying its structure?

| Situation | Diagnosis |
|-----------|-----------|
| Repetition created extra length | Expansion |
| Naturally long BI, no repetition | Non-standard length |
| Recomposition destroys structure | Not expansion |

**Example:** Mozart, String Quintet K. 515
- 5-bar compound basic ideas (3-bar BIs)
- No repetition → simply longer, not expanded

## Compositional Application

### When to Use Internal Expansion

1. **Delay expected cadence** — builds tension
2. **Compensate for weak cadence** — "try again"
3. **Add weight to conclusion** — emphasis
4. **Avoid predictability** — break 4+4 pattern

### When to Use External Expansion

| Type | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Prefix | Establish tempo, texture, mood before theme |
| Closing section | Confirm PAC, extend arrival |
| Standing on dominant | Build anticipation, signal arrival |

## Musical Examples Summary

| Technique | Example | Result |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| Sub-phrase expansion | Mozart K. 494 | 3-bar BI |

| Evaded cadence | Mozart K. 309 | 4-bar extension |
