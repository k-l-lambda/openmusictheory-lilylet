# Melodic Elaboration Overview

## Summary

Melodic elaboration refers to techniques for embellishing a melody using non-chord tones (NCTs). These tones add interest, expression, and forward motion to a melodic line while maintaining the underlying harmonic structure.

## Non-Chord Tones (NCTs)

Non-chord tones are pitches that do not belong to the current chord. They create tension that resolves to chord tones, adding expressiveness to melody.

### Classification System

NCTs are classified by:
1. **Metric position** — accented (strong beat) or unaccented (weak beat)
2. **Approach** — by step or by leap
3. **Resolution** — by step or by leap

## Overview of Types

| Type | Approach | Resolution | Metric Position |
|------|----------|------------|-----------------|
| Passing Tone (PT) | Step | Step (same dir.) | Either |
| Neighbor Tone (NT) | Step | Step (opposite) | Usually unaccented |
| Double Neighbor (DN) | Step (both dirs.) | Return to original | Unaccented |
| Incomplete Neighbor (INT) | Leap | Step | Unaccented |
| Appoggiatura (APP) | Leap | Step | Accented |
| Escape Tone (ESC) | Step | Leap | Unaccented |
| Anticipation (ANT) | Step | Same note | Unaccented |
| Suspension (SUS) | Common tone | Step down | Accented |
| Retardation (RET) | Common tone | Step up | Accented |
| Syncopation (SYN) | Tied | Tied | Weak → Strong |

## Key Concepts

### Stability and Tension

- **Chord tones** = stable
- **Non-chord tones** = tension (requires resolution)
- **Resolution** = return to stability

### Step vs. Leap

Most NCTs involve **stepwise motion** for smooth voice-leading. Exceptions (APP, ESC, INT) create special expressive effects.

### Metric Emphasis

- **Accented NCTs** (SUS, APP, accented PT/NT) create stronger tension
- **Unaccented NCTs** (most types) create passing/decorative effect
