# Prolongation

## Summary

Prolongation extends a harmonic function over multiple chords, creating zones of tonic, subdominant, or dominant that span more than a single chord. Contrapuntal chords (passing, neighbor, embellishing) prolong functions between cadential chords.

## Core Concept

### Functional Zones

In an idealized phrase, functions are not just single chords but *zones* of varying length:

```
T → (S) → D → T
```

### Chord Types

| Type | Bass | Role |
|------|------|------|
| **Functional/Cadential** | Fixed scale degree | Triggers function |
| **Contrapuntal/Embellishing** | Variable scale degree | Prolongs function |

### Functional Triggers

| Function | Trigger | Chords |
|----------|---------|--------|
| Tonic | T1 | I |
| Subdominant | S2 or S4 | ii, IV (any inversion) |
| Dominant | D5 | V, V7 |

## Prolongation Techniques

### 1. Change-of-Figure Prolongation

Bass repeats or sustains while upper voices change.

| Progression | Function | Bass |
|-------------|----------|------|
| V – V7 | D5 – D5 | sol – sol |
| IV – ii6 | S4 – S4 | fa – fa |
| I – I7 | T1 – T1 | do – do |

### 2. Change-of-Bass Prolongation

Two chords of same function with different bass notes.

| Progression | Function | Bass Motion |
|-------------|----------|-------------|
| I – I6 | T1 – T3 | do – mi |
| I – iii | T1 – T3 | do – mi |
| IV – ii | S4 – S2 | fa – re |

### 3. Passing Chord

Fills in a third in the bass with stepwise motion.

**Principle:** Function is prolonged by chords from the *preceding* function in the cycle.
- T prolonged by D
- D prolonged by S
- S prolonged by T

| Prolongation | Passing Chord | Progression |
|--------------|---------------|-------------|
| T1 – T3 | D2 | I – V6/4 – I6 |
| T1 – T3 | D2 | I – vii°6 – I6 |

**Common D2 chords:** V6/4, V4/3, vii°6

### 4. Incomplete Neighbor Chord

Substitutes leap-then-step for stepwise passing motion (like second-species substitution).

| Progression | Bass Motion | Example |
|-------------|-------------|---------|
| T1 – D4 – T3 | do – fa – mi | I – V4/2 – I6 |

### 5. Complete Neighbor Chord

Bass neighbor tone ornaments and returns to original pitch.

| Progression | Bass Motion | Example |
|-------------|-------------|---------|
| T1 – D7 – T1 | do – ti – do | I – V6/5 – I |
| T3 – D4 – T3 | mi – fa – mi | I6 – V4/2 – I6 |
| S4 – T3 – S4 | fa – mi – fa | IV – I6 – IV |
| D7 – S6 – D7 | ti – la – ti | V6/5 – IV6 – V6/5 |

### 6. Double Neighbor Chord

Two-chord neighbor figure in bass.

**Most common:** T1 – D2 – D7 – T1

| Progression | Bass | Example |
|-------------|------|---------|
| T1 – D2 – D7 – T1 | do – re – ti – do | I – V4/3 – V6/5 – I |

### 7. Divider Chord

Divides large leap (or octave) in bass into two smaller leaps.

| Progression | Bass | Example |
|-------------|------|---------|
| T1 – D5 – T1 | do – sol – do | I – V(7) – I |
| T1 – S4 – T1 | do – fa – do | I – IV – I |
| T1 – S6 – T3 | do – la – mi | I – IV6 – I6 |

**"Champagne progression":** I – IV6 – I6 (or I – IV6 – III)

### 8. Embellishing Chord

Like divider but bass returns to same octave (leap-return pattern).

| Progression | Bass | Example |
|-------------|------|---------|
| T1 – D5 – T1 | do – sol – do (same octave) | I – V – I |
| T1 – S4 – T1 | do – fa – do (same octave) | I – IV – I |

## Subsidiary Harmonic Progressions

When T–S–D–T fails to produce a proper cadence (ends with contrapuntal chords or deceptive resolution), it becomes a *subsidiary progression* that prolongs tonic.

**Criteria for subsidiary:**
- Ends D7–T1 (not D5–T1)
- Ends D4–T3 (not D5–T1)
- Uses D5–T6 (deceptive, V–vi)

**Notation:** T——— (line extends through entire subsidiary progression)

## Plagal Prolongations

S can prolong T in certain patterns:

| Progression | Type | Example |
|-------------|------|---------|
| I – IV6 – I6 | Champagne | S6 divider |
| I – IV – I6 | Neighbor | S4 incomplete neighbor |
| I6 – IV – I6 | Neighbor | S4 complete neighbor |
| I – IV6/4 – I | Change-of-figure | 6/4 as neighbor tones |

## Lament Bass

Special progression prolonging movement from T to D:

```
Bass: do – te – le – sol
Chords: i – v6 – iv6 – V (minor)
Functions: T1 → D7 → S6 → D5
```

The D7 (v6) is a passing chord connecting T1 to S6. Notation: arrow T → S.

## Compositional Application

### When to Use Prolongation

1. **Extend tonic zone** — Before moving to S or D
2. **Delay cadence** — Build anticipation
3. **Add melodic interest** — Bass motion creates variety
4. **Smooth voice-leading** — Stepwise bass connections

### Choosing Prolongation Type

| Goal | Technique |
|------|-----------|
| Stepwise bass | Passing, neighbor |
| Leaping bass | Divider, embellishing |
| Static bass | Change-of-figure |
| Multiple chords | Subsidiary progression |
