This section introduces students to the basics of Western music notation, including rhythm, pitch, and expressive markings. Students also learn to construct and identify rudimentary harmonies, including intervals (two-note harmonies), triads (three-note chords), and seventh chords (four-note chords).

## Prerequisites

The Fundamentals section assumes no previous familiarity with Western musical notation. However, each chapter in this section assumes familiarity with all preceding chapters; for that reason, it is recommended that chapters are studied in order.

## Organization

In the section’s first chapter, [Introduction to Western Musical Notation](01-introduction-to-western-musical-notation.md), students are encouraged to think about the ways in which they might write down their favorite song or composition. The next six chapters ([Notation of Notes, Clefs, and Ledger Lines](02-notation-of-notes-clefs-and-ledger-lines.md) through [Other Aspects of Notation](07-other-aspects-of-notation.md)) focus upon the notation of pitch and the expressive and stylistic conventions of Western musical notation.

Next, students are introduced to the conventions of Western rhythmic notation in the subsequent four chapters ([Notating Rhythm](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/rhythmic-rest-values/) through [Other Rhythmic Essentials](12-other-rhythmic-essentials.md)). Pitch is then revisited, beginning with the spelling and identification of scales, key signatures, the diatonic modes, and the chromatic collection (in [Major Scales, Scale Degrees, and Key Signatures](13-major-scales.md) through [Introduction to Diatonic Modes and the Chromatic “Scale”](15-intro-to-diatonic-modes-and-the-chromatic-scale.md)).

The following chapter, [The Basics of Sight-singing and Dictation](../14-ear-training/01-the-basics-of-sight-singing-and-dictation.md), presumes knowledge of the concepts in all previous chapters. It can be used as a stand-alone chapter in an aural skills class or within the context of a music theory or fundamentals course. Finally, the construction of harmonies is explored, from two-note [Intervals](17-intervals.md) through four-note [Seventh Chords](19-seventh-chords.md).

The [Triads](18-triads.md) and [Seventh Chords](19-seventh-chords.md) chapters do not include inversion or figured bass, as this is covered in a separate chapter ([Inversion and Figured Bass](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/inversion-and-figured-bass/)). This chapter, along with [Roman Numerals](21-roman-numerals.md), [Texture](08-texture.md), and [Chords in SATB Style](23-chords-in-satb-style.md) can be used as introductions to part-writing, counterpoint, music appreciation, or Western music history.

## Audience

The Fundamentals section is designed for a wide audience, including high school students (including those taking AP Music Theory), collegiate non-music majors (and musical theater majors), and collegiate music majors.
