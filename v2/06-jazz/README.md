This section introduces students to the basics of harmony, rhythm, and improvisation within traditional jazz and blues. Like any other equally broad genre, jazz encompasses more musical languages than are represented here, of course, but students will gain familiarity with idioms that structure subgenres such as swing and bebop.

## Prerequisites

This section assumes a familiarity with the topics covered in [Fundamentals](../01-fundamentals/README.md) as well as the [Tonicization](../03-diatonic-harmony/15-tonicization.md) chapter.

## Organization

The first chapter, [Swing Rhythms](01-swing-rhythms.md), introduces students to common and stylistic rhythms that are often found in swing music and subgenres that grew out of it.

Next, students learn about simple and complex [chord symbols](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/lead-sheet-symbols/) and how those symbols are idiomatically [voiced](03-jazz-voicings.md) in a jazz context.

The next few chapters discuss common harmonic formulas encountered in jazz repertories: [ii–V–I](04-ii-v-i.md); [embellishing chords](05-jazz-embellishing-chords.md) such as applied chords and common tone diminished seventh chords; and [substitutions](06-substitutions.md) such as the tritone substitution, mode mixture, and substituting applied chords.

Improvisation is discussed in the [Chord-Scale Theory](07-chord-scale-theory.md) chapter, which also introduces students to how jazz musicians use modes to improvise.

The final chapters of this section cover the [formal/harmonic](08-blues-harmony.md) and [melodic](09-blues-melodies-and-the-blues-scale.md) aspects of traditional blues music. This also serves as a transition into the following [section on popular music](../07-popular-music/README.md), a genre that borrows heavily from the blues.
