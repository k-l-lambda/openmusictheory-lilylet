This section introduces students to the basics of sight-singing, sight-counting, and dictations.

## Prerequisites

The Ear Training Appendix assumes familiarity with Western musical notation (i.e. the first fourteen chapters of the [Fundamentals](../01-fundamentals/README.md) section). The chapters in this section assume familiarity with all preceding chapters in the section; for that reason, it is recommended that chapters are studied in order.

## Organization

In the section’s first chapter, [The Basics of Sight-singing and Dictation](01-the-basics-of-sight-singing-and-dictation.md), students are introduced to strategies for sight-singing, such as using melodic solmization. Sight-counting and rhythmic solmization are also introduced. Students learn strategies for taking rhythmc and melodic dictation, including dot grids, slash notation, protonotation, and contour lines. The next two chapters, [Examples for Sight-counting and Sight-singing: Level 1](02-examples-for-sight-counting-and-sight-singing.md) and [Examples for Sight-counting and Sight-singing: Level 2](03-examples-for-sight-counting-and-sight-singing-level-2.md) introduce a series of gradated rhythms, melodies, two-part rhythms and two-part melodies, which can be used for practicing sight-counting and sight-singing.

## Audience

The Ear Training Appendix is designed for a wide audience, including high school students (including those taking AP Music Theory), collegiate non-music majors (and musical theater majors), and collegiate music majors.
